The Global Threat of Cardiovascular Disease (CVD)

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), cardiovascular diseases remain the leading cause of death globally, taking an estimated 19.8 million lives annually (roughly 32% of all global deaths). Over 85% of these fatalities are attributed directly to heart attacks and strokes.

Because early stages of vascular disease often present zero physical symptoms, machine learning screening tools analyze subtle electrophysiological, hemodynamic, and biochemical warning signals before acute clinical events happen.

Inner Heart Anatomy & Critical Disease Zones

Understanding how internal structural health ties directly into our machine learning model parameters:

Coronary Arteries & Major Vessels (ca)

Supply oxygenated blood to the heart muscle. Blockages or staining detected via fluoroscopy directly increase model risk coefficients.

Left Ventricle & Muscle Wall (restecg / oldpeak)

Pumps blood to the body. Hypertrophy or ST-segment depression signals abnormal workload and potential ischemic damage.

Myocardial Blood Flow (thal)

Thallium scans measure tissue perfusion; fixed or reversible perfusion defects highlight areas vulnerable to myocardial infarction.

Understanding the ML Screener Parameters & Healthy Baselines

Our model evaluates 13 key clinical features derived from standard UCI/Cleveland diagnostic guidelines. Below is an explanation of what each parameter measures, the threat profile of abnormal levels, and typical healthy target metrics:

1. Age & Sex (Demographics)

What it measures: Patient age in years and biological sex (1 = Male, 0 = Female).

Threat profile: Cardiovascular vulnerability naturally accumulates over time due to arterial stiffening. Post-menopausal women and men over 45 face significantly elevated relative risks.

Healthy Baseline Reference: Younger cohorts (< 45 years) exhibit baseline arterial elasticity, though risk is managed through overall metabolic health regardless of age.

2. Chest Pain Type (cp)

What it measures: Categorized chest discomfort types: 0 (Typical Angina), 1 (Atypical Angina), 2 (Non-anginal Pain), and 3 (Asymptomatic).

Threat profile: Typical angina (exertional chest discomfort relieved by rest) strongly correlates with coronary artery obstruction and myocardial ischemia.

Healthy Baseline Reference: Value 2 (Non-anginal pain or completely asymptomatic presentation with no cardiac restriction).

3. Resting Blood Pressure (trestbps) & Cholesterol (chol)

What it measures: Resting blood pressure upon hospital admission in mm Hg and serum cholesterol in mg/dl.

Threat profile: Chronic hypertension forces the heart muscle to work harder, thickening ventricles. High LDL cholesterol contributes directly to plaque build-up (atherosclerosis).

Healthy Baseline Reference: Resting BP < 120 mmHg; Serum Cholesterol < 200 mg/dl.

4. Fasting Blood Sugar (fbs)

What it measures: Evaluates if fasting blood sugar is greater than 120 mg/dl (1 = True, 0 = False).

Threat profile: Elevated blood sugar damages blood vessels and accelerates microvascular and macrovascular complications, heavily increasing heart attack susceptibility.

Healthy Baseline Reference: Value 0 (Fasting plasma glucose normal / under 100-120 mg/dl).

5. Resting ECG & Maximum Heart Rate (restecg & thalach)

What it measures: Electrical heart rhythm at rest (0: Normal, 1: ST-T wave abnormality, 2: Left Ventricular Hypertrophy) and peak heart rate achieved during stress testing.

Threat profile: ST-T wave irregularities indicate myocardial strain or current ischemic injury. Subpar peak heart rates reflect poor cardiac reserve.

Healthy Baseline Reference: Value 0 for restecg (Normal rhythm); higher peak heart rate capacity scaled to age during exertion.

6. Exercise Angina, Oldpeak, Slope, Vessels (ca), & Thalassemia (thal)

What it measures: Advanced clinical stress markers including exercise-induced chest pain, ST depression magnitude (oldpeak), fluoroscopy major vessel staining (0-3), and thallium scan blood perfusion defects.

Threat profile: Multiple colored obstructed vessels (ca > 0) or fixed/reversible thallium perfusion defects (thal = 6 or 7) signify physical coronary blockages requiring urgent clinical evaluation.

Healthy Baseline Reference: Exang = 0 (No induced angina), Oldpeak = 0.0, ca = 0 vessels blocked, thal = 3 (Normal blood flow).
