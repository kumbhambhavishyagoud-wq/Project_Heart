Abstarct

Heart disease is one of the most complex diseases, and globally, many people suffer from it. Timely and efficient identification of heart disease plays a key role in healthcare, particularly in cardiology. In this article, we propose an efficient and accurate system to diagnose heart disease based on machine learning techniques. The system is developed using classification algorithms, including Support Vector Machine, Logistic regression, Artificial neural network, K-nearest neighbour, Naïve Bayes, and Decision tree, while standard feature selection algorithms such as Relief, Minimal redundancy maximal relevance, Least absolute shrinkage selection operator, and Local learning are used to remove irrelevant and redundant features. We also propose a novel fast conditional mutual information feature selection algorithm to solve the feature selection problem. The feature selection algorithms are used to select features, increase classification accuracy, and reduce the execution time of the classification system. Furthermore, the leave-one-subject-out cross-validation method has been used for learning best practices in model assessment and for hyperparameter tuning. The performance metrics are used to assess the performance of the classifiers. The performance of the classifiers has been evaluated on the features selected by the feature selection algorithms. The experimental results show that the proposed feature selection algorithm (FCMIM) is feasible with the Support Vector Machine classifier for designing a high-level intelligent system to identify heart disease. The suggested diagnosis system (FCMIM-SVM) achieved good accuracy compared to previously proposed methods. Additionally, the proposed system can easily be implemented in healthcare for the identification of heart disease

ML Model

This application utilizes a Random Forest Classifier, an advanced ensemble machine learning algorithm that constructs multiple decision trees during training and outputs the majority class prediction. Random Forest is chosen for this clinical screener because it excels at handling complex, non-linear medical relationships among 13 distinct cardiac parameters. Its primary advantages include high predictive accuracy, robustness against overfitting, and the ability to seamlessly handle missing values. Crucially, it prevents individual tree bias, ensuring reliable risk intelligence when balancing subtle hemodynamic markers and severe ischemic warning signs.

Parameters

Complete Feature & Parameter Guide

Detailed breakdown of the 13 clinical variables analyzed by the ML prediction model.

1. Age
Patient Age (Years)

Measures: Chronological age of the patient.

Threat: Arterial stiffening and plaque accumulation naturally progress over time.

Healthy Baseline: Younger cohorts (< 45 years) exhibit lower baseline vascular risk.

2. Sex
Biological Sex

Measures: 1 = Male, 0 = Female.

Threat: Men generally face earlier onset risks; post-menopausal women experience sharp increases in cardiovascular vulnerability.

Healthy Baseline: Monitored relative to age and metabolic profile.

3. cp
Chest Pain Type (0–3)

Measures: 0: Typical Angina, 1: Atypical Angina, 2: Non-anginal Pain, 3: Asymptomatic.

Threat: Type 0 (Typical angina triggered by exertion and relieved by rest) strongly correlates with coronary blockage.

Healthy Baseline: Type 2 (Non-anginal) or Type 3 (Asymptomatic).

4. trestbps
Resting Blood Pressure (mmHg)

Measures: Blood pressure upon hospital admission.

Threat: Chronic hypertension forces the heart to work harder, thickening the left ventricle and damaging arteries.

Healthy Baseline: < 120 mmHg.

5. chol
Serum Cholesterol (mg/dl)

Measures: Total circulating cholesterol levels in the blood.

Threat: High LDL cholesterol promotes fatty plaque build-up inside coronary arteries (atherosclerosis).

Healthy Baseline: < 200 mg/dl.

6. fbs
Fasting Blood Sugar > 120

Measures: 1 = True (glucose > 120 mg/dl), 0 = False.

Threat: High blood sugar accelerates microvascular damage and increases systemic inflammation and heart attack risk.

Healthy Baseline: 0 (Normal fasting glucose).

7. restecg
Resting ECG Results (0–2)

Measures: 0: Normal, 1: ST-T Wave Abnormality, 2: Left Ventricular Hypertrophy.

Threat: Abnormal ST-T waves or ventricular enlargement indicate myocardial strain or past cardiac injury.

Healthy Baseline: 0: Normal electrical rhythm.

8. thalach
Max Heart Rate Achieved (bpm)

Measures: Peak heart rate recorded during physical stress testing.

Threat: Unusually low maximum heart rate capacity can signal poor cardiovascular reserve or chronotropic incompetence.

Healthy Baseline: Higher age-adjusted peak heart rate capacity.

9. exang
Exercise Induced Angina

Measures: 1 = Yes, 0 = No.

Threat: Experiencing chest pain specifically during physical exertion indicates oxygen deprivation in the heart muscle.

Healthy Baseline: 0 (No exercise-induced angina).

10. oldpeak
Oldpeak (ST Depression)

Measures: Magnitude of ST-segment depression induced by exercise relative to rest.

Threat: Higher depression values (e.g., > 2.0) strongly correlate with severe myocardial ischemia.

Healthy Baseline: 0.0 (No ST depression).

11. slope
Peak Exercise ST Slope (0–2)

Measures: 0: Upsloping, 1: Flat, 2: Downsloping.

Threat: Flat or downsloping ST segments during stress testing are key indicators of coronary artery disease.

Healthy Baseline: 0: Upsloping.

12. ca
Major Vessels Colored (0–3)

Measures: Number of major coronary vessels (0 to 3) colored/obstructed via fluoroscopy.

Threat: More blocked vessels drastically increase ischemic risk and likelihood of heart failure.

Healthy Baseline: 0 Vessels blocked.

13. thal
Thalassemia Blood Flow Defect (3, 6, 7)

Measures: 3: Normal blood flow, 6: Fixed Defect (permanent damage), 7: Reversible Defect (ischemia under stress).

Threat: Fixed or reversible perfusion defects highlight areas of the heart muscle vulnerable to infarction.

Healthy Baseline: 3: Normal perfusion.
